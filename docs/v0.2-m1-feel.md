# I AM BISON — v0.2 M1 FEEL Specification

## 1. Purpose

v0.2 M1 is a **game-feel pass**.

The current prototype already contains the main gameplay systems. This milestone does not add a new game mode, progression system, or major mechanic.

The goal is narrower:

> Make moving a herd feel powerful, heavy, readable, and satisfying even before final art exists.

This pass should improve the moment-to-moment experience of:

- moving forward
- steering
- growing the herd
- carrying a large herd
- breaking through a fence
- losing control

The game should feel better even if every bison remains a primitive circle.

---

## 2. Scope rule

Implement **FEEL improvements only**.

Do not add:

- MOMENTUM / STAMPEDE meter
- route-choice generation
- new biomes
- cars
- buildings
- progression
- unlocks
- currency
- upgrades
- new game modes
- final artwork
- backend
- leaderboard
- save data

Those belong to later milestones.

---

## 3. Design principle

The core fantasy is not "I control one fast character."

It is:

> I am steering a growing living mass that becomes more powerful and harder to control.

Every change in this milestone should strengthen at least one of these sensations:

1. **Mass**
2. **Momentum**
3. **Growth**
4. **Impact**
5. **Loss of control**

Do not add effects merely because they look decorative.

---

## 4. Camera feel

### 4.1 Dynamic zoom

The camera should smoothly zoom out as the active herd grows.

The purpose is:

- make herd growth visually obvious
- keep a larger portion of the herd readable
- make a large herd feel physically larger

Suggested behavior:

- small herd: closer camera
- medium herd: moderately wider view
- very large herd: wider view, but never so far out that bison become unreadable

Use a continuous curve rather than hard size tiers if practical.

All zoom values must be configurable.

Example tuning targets only:

- 1–5 bison: ~1.00
- 20 bison: ~0.92
- 50 bison: ~0.82
- 100 bison: ~0.72

Do not snap between zoom levels.

Smoothly interpolate toward the target zoom.

### 4.2 Forward look-ahead

The camera should look slightly ahead of the herd in its current heading.

The player should see more of the route they are moving toward than the area behind them.

Requirements:

- camera target remains based on the herd/leader position
- add configurable forward offset based on heading
- smooth the target movement
- avoid sudden jumps during sharp turns

The effect should improve anticipation without making steering feel detached.

---

## 5. Turning feel

The existing steering model should remain recognizable.

Do not replace the herd model with a new physics engine.

Improve the feeling that a turn travels through the herd rather than every member changing direction uniformly.

Desired visual:

1. the front commits to the turn
2. nearby followers start bending
3. the rear reacts later
4. the formation stretches
5. the herd gradually settles again

Possible implementation approaches:

- slightly vary alignment response based on distance from leader
- slightly vary response based on relative position along the heading axis
- use existing per-bison agility as part of the effect
- smooth heading influence rather than introducing rigid constraints

Avoid:

- rubber-band snapping
- extreme tail whip
- followers orbiting the leader
- obvious artificial delays
- oscillation after a turn

### Acceptance check

A continuous left-right slalom should visibly bend the herd without making control feel random.

---

## 6. Speed and ground feedback

The player should feel forward movement even when no obstacle is nearby.

Add lightweight ground feedback.

Recommended:

### Dust

Generate subtle dust behind moving bison.

Requirements:

- intensity scales with active herd size
- emission should be capped for performance
- large herd = broader / denser dust, not one particle system per bison if that becomes expensive
- dust should not obscure obstacle readability
- use primitive particles / generated textures; no final art required

### Optional micro motion

A very subtle camera micro-bob or vibration may scale with herd size.

It must remain much weaker than impact shake.

If it makes mobile play uncomfortable or harms readability, omit it.

---

## 7. Recruitment feedback

Recruiting wild bison must feel rewarding immediately.

When one or more bison join:

- briefly show "+N"
- use a small visual pulse or ring around the joining area or herd
- optionally add a very small camera response
- update HERD immediately

If several bison join in the same short window, group the feedback rather than spamming multiple overlapping messages.

The effect should communicate:

> The herd just got bigger.

It should not pause gameplay.

---

## 8. Impact feedback

### 8.1 Fence destruction

When a breakable fence is destroyed:

- brief camera shake
- visible fence breakup/reaction
- small particle/debris burst using primitives
- optional short hit-stop of only a few milliseconds if it feels good

Avoid long freezes.

The herd should feel like it drove through the object rather than stopped to play an effect.

### 8.2 Non-breakable collision

When a collision causes disruption/loss:

- feedback should be different from successful destruction
- use a sharper, less triumphant visual response
- avoid excessive shake that hides why the collision happened

The player should be able to distinguish:

- successful power
- failed impact / mistake

---

## 9. Herd-size presentation

Herd growth should change the atmosphere before final art/audio exists.

At minimum, size should affect multiple presentation layers:

- camera zoom
- dust amount
- optional micro vibration
- formation width / visual footprint through existing herd behavior

Do not make the only sign of growth the HUD number.

---

## 10. Audio hooks

Do not source final music or sound assets in this milestone.

However, structure the code so later audio can react to herd size.

If lightweight procedural/browser-generated sound can be added without derailing the milestone, a minimal prototype may include:

- sparse hoof pulse for small herd
- denser rhythm for medium herd
- low rumble layer for large herd
- short impact sound for fence break

This is optional for M1 acceptance.

Do not block completion on final audio quality.

---

## 12. Performance

The project targets desktop and mobile browsers.

Game-feel effects must not destroy frame stability.

Guidelines:

- cap particle counts
- avoid allocating large temporary arrays every frame
- reuse Phaser particle systems where practical
- do not create one expensive effect system per bison
- preserve the existing delta clamp
- effects should degrade gracefully at 100+ bison

The herd simulation remains more important than decorative effects.

---

## 13. Development controls

Keep the existing test herd size shortcuts if present.

They are useful for comparing:

- small herd
- medium herd
- large herd

Add temporary dev readouts only if needed for tuning.

Do not build a polished debug menu in this milestone.

---

## 14. Manual evaluation

After implementation, test at minimum:

### Test A — Small herd

Start with ~5 bison.

Check:

- camera is relatively close
- movement is readable
- dust is restrained
- steering feels agile

### Test B — Medium herd

Use ~20–50 bison.

Check:

- camera has pulled back smoothly
- herd visually bends during turns
- forward motion feels heavier
- dust/ground feedback is clearly stronger

### Test C — Large herd

Use ~100 bison.

Check:

- herd still fits/readably occupies the view
- camera does not zoom excessively far out
- game remains performant
- movement feels powerful but controllable
- effects do not hide rocks/fences/rivers

### Test D — Recruitment

Recruit a small group.

Check:

- the player immediately notices the gain
- "+N" is readable
- feedback does not interrupt steering

### Test E — Fence impact

Break a fence with a sufficiently large herd.

Check:

- impact feels substantially better than before
- the game does not pause noticeably
- successful destruction is visually distinct from a failed collision

---

## 15. Acceptance criteria

v0.2 M1 is complete when:

- camera zoom responds smoothly to herd size
- camera provides readable forward look-ahead
- turns visually propagate through the herd more naturally
- forward movement has lightweight ground/dust feedback
- recruitment has clear positive feedback
- fence destruction has satisfying but controlled impact feedback
- failed impacts remain distinguishable from successful destruction
- all important feel values are centralized for tuning
- performance remains acceptable with ~100 bison
- no M2+ gameplay mechanic has been added

---

## 16. Stop condition

**STOP after v0.2 M1.**

Do not implement route-choice systems or MOMENTUM/STAMPEDE yet.

After this pass, the build must be manually played and tuned before the next milestone.

The question to answer is:

> Does the existing game become noticeably more enjoyable to control without adding a new core mechanic?
